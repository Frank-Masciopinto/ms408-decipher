# FINDINGS — what this repository did, found, and did not find

> **Beinecke MS 408 (the Voynich manuscript) remains undeciphered.** Nothing in this repository is a translation, a plaintext candidate, or a decipherment claim. Everything below is a *description* of one transcription (Zandbergen–Landini ZL 3b, IVTFF 2.0, running text unless stated), a comparison with control texts and generators, or a test of someone else's claim. All numbers are taken from the reports linked in each row; where a number is a point estimate from a fitted or simplified generator it says so. Last updated 14 Oct 2026.

## 1. Executive summary

1. **MS 408 is still undeciphered, and none of the ~33 stages here changes that.** The work produced measurements, nulls and tests, not a reading.
2. **Firmly established (descriptive, survives controls and CIs):** letter-level conditional entropy is far below every control family (h2 2.217 ± 0.092 and h3 1.954 ± 0.079 bits, vs ≈ 3.0–3.9 for Latin/Italian/German/Hebrew/Arabic; natural-text z(h2) = −6.8; not explained by alphabet size, z = −5.9).
3. **Also established:** words are unusually position-bound — shuffling units inside words costs the slot grammar 2.93 bits/type (CI 2.66–3.19) vs a natural-language median 0.60 and maximum 1.47 — yet words are prefix-heavy and suffix-light (21 % of types with a learned suffix vs 0.67–0.73 in natural text).
4. **Not discriminating:** Zipf (s 0.985), Heaps (β 0.745), type/token (0.205), word entropy and the like fall inside natural-language *and* many cipher/generator intervals; "it obeys Zipf" says nothing here.
5. **Section vocabulary differs, but "beyond drift" is no longer established:** naive-Bayes classification of section reaches 0.723 balanced accuracy (0.94 / 0.98 within Currier A / B). With folio resampling that was above the drift-null max 0.611 (CI 0.676–0.766). With **quire** resampling (h08) the CI is 0.523–0.924, so it is **no longer above the drift null**. Leak-free bifolium folds give 0.670. Sections and quires nearly coincide (biological = Q13 only). **h10** holds quire and hand fixed. Within one quire and hand (Q15, hand 1 A), herbal and pharmaceutical pages still differ (JS excess 0.072 bits, BA 0.83, Holm p = 0.010 under stratified permutation), but too few pages exist for the drift-aware null to confirm it. Within a section, hand/language effects are 3–4× larger (herbal hand 1 A vs 2 B in the same quires: JS excess 0.222, BA 0.99). In the variance decomposition, unique section R² is 0.025, against quire 0.068, hand 0.015, language 0.005, with 0.200 of 0.323 shared. Most of the apparent section signal is hand/language plus quire; a residual section effect is real in direction but not established. **h11** models hand × language explicitly. It is the strongest single factor (held-out gain 0.109 bits/token with quires held out; section adds 0.015 more). On hand residuals a section signal survives both within-hand nulls (all sections pseudo-F 2.32, Holm 0.005 / 0.047; herbal vs pharma BA 0.86), but it vanishes entirely once quire is also removed. Section beyond hand cannot be told apart from quire beyond hand.
6. **Candidate mechanisms (Naibbe cipher, Timm–Schinner self-citation, Zattera/Stolfi grammars, hybrids) each match some metrics and miss others;** a fitted self-citation generator reproduces 23/35 and a Naibbe + copy-modify hybrid 21.7/35 held out, but no model of any kind reproduces the suffix rate, and the plaintext ingredient adds nothing measurable over a no-plaintext copy-modify control.
7. **The section signal does not require plaintext:** section-varying generators with *no* plaintext (a 10–50-word seed pool per section, 66–306 parameters) reach NB accuracy 0.68–0.72; random-block and language-only versions do not (0.28–0.36). A topic-driven plaintext is sufficient, not necessary. With quire CIs, MS 408's between-section JS (0.406, CI 0.276–0.537) is no longer above the best no-plaintext generator (0.357). In a leave-one-section-out test (h07), **no model predicts an unseen section well** (7–16 of 50 cells inside the 95 % interval, no better than a global no-section baseline). The setting-switch generator beats the topic codebook (mean |z| 4.70 vs 6.31, p = 0.0098), but topical Latin is no better than random Latin, and the "nearest" settings are no better than random ones.
8a. **Drawings as cribs (h09, rechecked in h10):** words enriched on bath, star, root and jar pages are all section/quire effects. Within quires, 0 words per tag survive Holm correction. Same-plant herbal–pharmaceutical pairs share no more vocabulary than chance (p = 0.57). The only weak leads are shared label endings by object type within quires (Holm p = 0.03) and pharmaceutical "root" words transferring to large-root herbal pages (p = 0.007, but about 0.056 after Holm and sensitive to the word-set size). Under a fixed hand (h10), only the pharmaceutical root/jar words beat frequency-matched random words (hand 1 A, log rate ratio 2.32, Holm 0.004). The water and star words do not (Holm 0.40). The root transfer weakens to p = 0.037 (Holm 0.099) within hand 1 A and is absent in hand 2 B. On hand residuals (h11) all three word sets remain enriched against permutation and random-word nulls (Holm 0.01) but none passes the within-hand drift null (Holm 0.09–0.26). With quire also removed, nothing remains.
8. **Ruled out or unsupported:** the "Codex Translation Sheet" as a translation table (h01); any extension of its tones to the whole script (h02, h03); "slot = syllable" (not diagnostic); sign/month names as cribs on the zodiac pages (h05 — the circle-centre words are later **Roman-script month names**, not Voynichese); a zodiac-local copy pool; calendar structure beyond page-order drift (h06).
8b. **Hands look like one system with different spelling habits and vocabularies (h11).** Ten greedy rewrite rules close 22.7 % of the herbal hand 1 A vs 2 B gap (first rule `-edy → -ol`, i.e. *chedy → chol*), against 1–11 % of within-hand section gaps and ≈ 0 for split halves. About 77 % of the hand gap is not closed by simple substitutions. In h12, per-hand generators beat a pooled generator on held-out text of their hand (top-word JS lower in 14/15 folds), but the fitted copy probability is ≈ 0.02–0.04: the gain comes from the per-hand word grammar, not from copying. Rewriting a 2 B generator's output with ≤ 10 learned rules (first rule `-edy → -ol`) closes 45–49 % of its top-100-word gap to hand 1 A, and 59–69 % in the 2 B ← 1 A direction. At the word level (h13), the rule pairs do not share neighbour contexts across hands (cosine 0.056 vs 0.054 for random pairs, p = 0.59). The mapping is a distributional correspondence, not a word-for-word one. Neighbour classes of the top 100 words mostly follow hand×language, and no top word has an image link that survives a quire×HL null. h14 qualifies this: after an orthogonal Procrustes alignment of the 2B and 1A context spaces, rule partners are closer than random (median 76th percentile, p = 0.0005), so there is some word-level correspondence. Line-start and line-end word preferences (daiin/dain/saiin/sain/sar/sol/y at the start, mostly in 2B/3B; am/dam/dy at the end, in all hands) survive line-length, paragraph and gap controls. h15 shows the line-initial preference is homogeneous across 2B/3B quires and bifolia (ratio 3.3, I² = 0.10) and genuinely absent in 1A (power 1.00). Most of the Procrustes rule-pair advantage disappears against same-stem words (percentile 0.60, p = 0.097), so word-level evidence for the -edy/-ol mapping is weak. h16 shows the 2B/3B line-start habit is largely an added onset: initial words are disproportionately d-/s- (or gallows) forms of common same-hand bases (dshedy, dchedy, tchedy, pchedy, dar, sor), while 1A has no onset excess and only a small set of its own line-start words (dchor, sor, sho). Line-final -am/-dy words occur in every hand.
8c. **Published plant identifications as cribs (h17).** I collected 239 tentative IDs for 107 herbal folios (Petersen/O'Neill/Holm, Ethel Voynich, Zandbergen, Tucker & Talbert, Vatne, Sherwood/Bax). The most agreed is f9v *Viola* (6 sources). Pharmaceutical labels for plants matched to a herbal folio are no more similar to that folio's text than to other folios in the same quire and hand (p ≥ 0.21). Same-genus or same-family folios do not share first words or vocabulary (p ≥ 0.16). Bax's crib words resemble paragraph-initial words on other folios of the same plant family (Holm 0.004), but almost all of that comes from one crib (`keerodal` ~ `ksheodal`, Apiaceae). The cribs were chosen post hoc, so this is a lead for an out-of-sample test, not a reading. **h18 ran that test and it failed:** on non-Bax Apiaceae folios, with the discovery folios excluded, none opens with a k…dal/k…dy-type word (0/3 vs null 0.17; token share p = 0.23).
8d. **Held-out likelihood (h19).** Under the same 10 k-token budget, real Latin/Italian texts gain 0.3–1.6 bits/word from word context and their best model is copy + trigram. MS 408 cells gain only 0.23–0.27, all of it from a cache of similar recent words. Word n-grams make predictions worse, and a context-free character model is best. Naibbe ciphertext is recognised by its own likelihood (−0.9 to −1.0 bits/word, escape ≤ 1 %), but MS 408 is not (escape 94–99 %). The ML copy rate (0.10–0.13) is well identified but lies within the range of real texts (0.07–0.17). It is not a fingerprint of mechanical copying, except perhaps at very short range (W = 5, exploratory).
8e. **Generators vs Italian (h20).** None of nine Italian texts reproduces the h19 fingerprint, including 14th-century prose, a ricettario and Cennini's workshop recipes. Neither does Italian passed through a word-preserving syllabic cipher, vowel removal or scribal suspension: all keep a word n-gram gain of +0.25 to +1.42 bits/word, where MS 408 has −0.13 to −0.18. Naibbe-enciphered Italian loses the n-gram gain but has almost no local repetition (5-word copy rate 0.007 vs 0.03–0.04). The h12 per-hand copy-modify engine reproduces all five statistics (it was fitted to MS on other statistics, so this is partly by construction). Self-citation comes close (≤ 4/5 on bifolium folds).
8f. **Order-breaking Italian and cross-hand C0 (h21).** Within-line shuffling does remove Italian's n-gram gain (D1 −0.15 to −0.24, MS-like), but char-model dominance and bits/glyph stay far off. Homophones and up to 41 % nulls (drawn from MS tokens) don't fix that, and Naibbe-Italian plus nulls reaches only 3/5 because its local copy rate stays too low. No setting of up to 4 layers reproduces the fingerprint on bifolium folds. The C0 engine fitted on other hands still lands in the MS range (4–5/5), so h20's match is not purely circular, but it does not reliably reproduce a specific unseen hand's values (2B never ≥ 4/5 when 2B was not used to fit).
8g. **Italian cipher plus copying (h22).** Adding a small generic copy-and-modify layer to Naibbe-Italian (5 % of tokens, window 20, not fitted to MS) gets 4/5 on all four Italian sources, at distance 5.4–7.9, close to the cross-hand C0 range of 1.9–6.0. The one remaining miss is word-vs-char dominance (D4 ≈ −0.17), which copying itself worsens; no setting reaches 5/5 on bifolium folds. h21's null benefit came largely from drawing the nulls from MS tokens: clean nulls cost about 20–234 distance units. Such an encoding would keep about 95 % of tokens as plain ciphertext, so the plaintext signal would largely survive. Over 5 seeds, C0 fits that never saw hand 2B never match it at ≥ 4/5 (D1 and bits/glyph miss every time), while blind fits match 1A every time. This points to a real difference in hand 2B.
8h. **Copy-rule variants and hand 2B (h23).** The rule used for copying matters. A suffix-swap copy (5 % of tokens: a recent token is copied with its final unit replaced, as in -edy → -ol) on Naibbe-Italian reproduces all five statistics on Boccaccio, at distance 3.0. Transferred to Cucina, Sidrach and Cennini it gets 4/5, at distance 3.0–3.7, missing only D4 and only just (−0.07 to −0.10 against the bound of −0.07). A within-line copy at 3 % gives 5/5 on Cennini. Copying whole words is what lowers word-vs-character dominance. These distances lie inside the MS-fitted C0 engine's cross-hand range (1.9–6.0), so the h19/h20 fingerprint does not separate a natural-language cipher plus scribal copying from a process generator. That does not show MS 408 is Italian. Naibbe is MS-inspired, and about 95 % of tokens stay decodable. Hand 2B's low bits/glyph sits in its biological pages (1.68, against 1.95 for its non-biological pages) and in the -edy family: removing -edy raises it by 0.12. This is confounded with section.
8i. **Neutral-cipher robustness (h24).** Over 5 seeds × 4 sources, Naibbe-Italian plus a light copy layer reaches ≥ 4/5 in all 60 cells (distance 2.2–4.4, mean 3.2–3.6). It reaches 5/5 in only 15–35 % of cells, all on Boccaccio and Cennini; Cucina and Sidrach just miss D4. Six cipher families not built from MS (homophonic substitution, verbose, nomenclator, with or without nulls and copying, in a made-up alphabet) never exceed 3/5, at distances ≥ 89. Homophonic ciphers spend too many bits per glyph (3.5 against 1.8); verbose and nomenclator ciphers keep word n-gram gain and word-model dominance. So h23's match depends on Naibbe's MS-like tables, not on 'any cipher plus copying'. Hand 2B's low bits/glyph is a section effect: 2B-herbal is within 0.02 of 1A-herbal, and only 2B-biological is low (1.68).
8j. **Neutral hybrid cipher (h25).** Naibbe's design with random made-up-glyph tables (respacing, card-drawn homophonic tables, prefix + suffix words) comes much closer than other neutral ciphers: mean distance 9–19, against ≥ 89, with 4/5 in some seeds. It never reaches 5/5 in 138 cells. The copy layers that made real Naibbe match make it worse, because random codes already collide and give MS-level repetition. In EVA-like units, bits/glyph matches, but word-vs-character dominance collapses (D4 −0.75 to −2.6). What carries the Naibbe match is the internal glyph structure of its MS-derived tables, not the design. Homophony (number of tables) is the most influential structural parameter, then code length.
8k. **Glyph-grammar tables (h26).** I filled the Naibbe engine's tables from a word-internal glyph grammar: random slot grammars, a Latin-spelling glyph chain, and, as a positive control, a chain trained on MS 2B. None of these reproduces the fingerprint (best mean distance 25, 0/5 seeds at ≥ 4/5). Even the MS-trained chain lands at 94–103; the main miss is word-vs-character dominance (D4 ≈ −0.8). With the author's real Naibbe tables, the same engine gives 3.43 (≥ 4/5 in 5/5 seeds). So the Naibbe match depends on its hand-designed tables, not on structure plus any orderly grammar. Rendered word length is the property most tied to closeness (ρ = +0.85). The closest neutral tables use short codes and are poorly decodable (0.2–0.4).
8l. **Plaintext removed (h27).** The author's tables and the 5% suffix-swap copy stay fixed. Real Boccaccio, the same words in random order, and Zipf nonsense (Italian letter frequencies, no Italian words) all land on the fingerprint: bifolium distance 3.43, 3.29 and 2.81, each ≥ 4/5 in 5/5 seeds. The real arm reproduces the h26 anchor seed for seed. So this match does not require Italian plaintext, Italian word order, or Italian morphology. It does not show that MS 408 is meaningless, and the +2 pass margin is wide relative to the seed scatter.
8m. **Table permutations (h28).** Three cuts of the real tables, same engine and copy as h27. Shuffling strings across letters within each slot gives distance 22.0; pooling prefix and suffix strings gives 27.5; shuffling EVA units inside each string gives 143.1. None is inside the match rule (distance ≤ 5.43 and ≥ 4/5 in ≥ 60% of seeds). Letter and state still reach ≥ 4/5 in 4/5 seeds, and the miss is D4 every time (about −0.39 and −0.45). The unit shuffle also misses D4 (−1.12) and often D5. So the bag of Voynichese-looking strings is not enough: which letter owns which string, which strings are prefixes versus suffixes, and the order inside each string all move the fingerprint, and the order moves it most.
8n. **D4 toys (h29).** On the stored h26 cells, mean word length remains the strongest correlate of distance (ρ +0.85), and it moves against D4 (ρ −0.81). A 5×5 toy of code length L and suffix-pool size V, with no copy layer and character segmentation, does reach D4 ≥ −0.07 at decodable share ≥ 0.8 (three cells: L=3, V=64 and 128, and L=4, V=128). Those D4 values are +0.38 to +0.48, above the MS window of about −0.07 to +0.18, so none of them is a two-sided D4 match. No toy cell is 5/5. The closest are L=2 with V=16 or 32 (4/5, distance 12.7 and 16.8), and both are under 0.51 decodable. Pool size does not pull D4 down (ρ +0.11).
8o. **Line structure (h30).** Lines in 1A, 2B and 3B are closed units: dependencies stop at the line break, words are marked by first/last position (3–4× hard-wrapped prose) and paragraph-first lines open with gallows. But they are not verse (no rhyme, prose-like length variance), and position adds only 0.03–0.04 bits/token to held-out prediction, so the pre-registered list/table label is not granted.
8d. **Word construction (h31).** Unsupervised segmentation (Morfessor, Bayesian, affix model) finds a generator-sized piece inventory: 0.07–0.085 morphs per word type, vs 0.06–0.07 for Naibbe/C0 and 0.21–0.54 for Latin, Italian, Hebrew and Arabic. Pieces combine more freely than in language, and morph models trail a character model by only 1.3–1.4 bits/word (natural 2.7–3.7). Neither the morpheme hypothesis nor the decoration hypothesis passes. The one language-like trait is a word-to-word junction dependence (ending or last letter → next word's first letter, 0.07–0.23 bits) that no tested generator reproduces.
8p. **Hand spelling system (h32).** On section-matched herbal text, 30 context-dependent unit rules close 29.3 % of the 1A vs 2B word-distribution gap (h11: 22.7 %; permuted hands ≤ 7 %), so hands share one system with a few word-edge habits (`-edy→-ol`, `#ai→dai`, `#a→cho`). 70.7 % of the gap remains, and after mapping the hands show no parallel passages, no extra shared n-grams and no same-plant-family similarity. The evidence points to different content, not the same text in different spelling.
8q. **Transcription targets (h40).** Across ZL3b (uncertain spaces split or joined), IT2a and RF1b, every hand's D1–D5 range stays inside the h20 tolerance (largest move is 2B's D4, 0.065 against a tolerance of 0.10). The last-glyph link stays between 0.083 and 0.242, against generator originals ≤ 0.0034. The space-predictability band does move. For 2B and 3B the transcription range sits entirely above the old 0.72–0.80 target (2B 0.811–0.827, 3B 0.815–0.847). Four of the nine h39 graph cells fall in that widened band, and three fall in the old one. The fingerprint target is stable. The space target used in h35–h39 was not.
8r. **Plaintext visibility (h41).** At 10,500 tokens, burstiness of the 50 most frequent words separates plain Boccaccio (4.16–4.78) from both shuffled and Zipf Naibbe ciphertext (both ≤ 3.74). New-type rate, lag-100 mutual information and adjacent-block divergence do not. That test compared plain text with ciphertext. Through the cipher, real Boccaccio is indistinguishable from shuffled or nonsense plaintext on all four statistics (burstiness 3.41–3.65, against 3.40–3.70 and 3.38–3.74). So no statistic tested sees a plaintext through Naibbe: the cipher flattens word clustering whatever goes in. The three MS hands score 5.22, 5.11 and 5.10, above plain Boccaccio (mean 4.40) and far above every Naibbe arm and C0 (at most 3.74). On this measure MS 408 does not look like Naibbe-class ciphertext of anything. It does not identify a language.
8s. **Near-repeat decay (h42).** One-edit pairs are not a property of Boccaccio (no bin elevated; adjacent excess −0.027) and not a property of the h39 graph respelling (no bin elevated). Hand 1A stays elevated through gap 6–20 and drops at 21–100. Hand 3B stays elevated through 21–100 and drops at 101–500. Hand 2B's adjacent pair is not elevated (excess 0.007); it is elevated at gaps 2–20, not at 21–100, and elevated again at 101–500 (excess 0.010). The pre-registered label uses the first drop, so 2B is called page-scaled at 21–100. C0 drops at gap 2–5, with a smaller elevation again at 6–20. Fitted self-citation and the h39 tuned copier both stay elevated through 21–100 and drop at 101–500. Under that first-drop rule the tuned copier outlasts hand 2B, and all three predictions pass. The second hump in hand 2B means the first drop is not the end of its excess.
8t. **Page edge (h43).** At a matched distance of 80 tokens, same-folio windows are more alike than windows split by a folio boundary in hands 1A (effect 0.082, null 95th percentile 0.059, 184 same / 45 cross) and 2B (0.100 vs 0.074, 4440 / 41). Hand 3B has only 23 cross pairs, so it is unscored. Of the three controls with real section edges, only Pliny's chapters pass at that distance (0.047 vs 0.014). Boccaccio's novellas and the recipe book do not have 30 cross pairs at distance 80. At distance 160, Boccaccio and Pliny pass and the recipe book does not (effect −0.26). The folio boundary is a real vocabulary break in 1A and 2B. It is not the same break as a novella or a recipe.
8u. **Generator page clustering (h44).** The burst excess of the three hands is 1.51, 1.69 and 1.50, and in every hand more of it sits inside the folio than inside the quire. Fitted self-citation, cut into arbitrary pages of 230 tokens, reaches 1.63. The h39 tuned copier reaches 4.76. C0 (0.15), an h06 section pool (0.27 with a pool, 0.05 without), and the h39 graph respelling (0.19) do not. A stream given a new vocabulary on every page reaches 33.2. So the excess number is not evidence of content: a local generator with no pages of its own can match it when the pages are drawn on afterwards. The folio-boundary test in h43 is the one that asks whether the real page edge matters.
8v. **Vowel/consonant share of the glyph link (h45).** Sukhotin recovers the vowels of the controls, and the two-way class split carries almost none of their last-to-first link. The median control share is 0.004, against a pre-registered bar of 0.25. Prose is under 0.006. Macer's verse is 0.21. The test of pen versus sound is not scored. MS shares are 0.058, 0.066 and 0.057, on a larger glyph-level link (0.17–0.28 bits) than the prose controls (0.06–0.14). Self-citation and the glyph-table gap rule are 0.002 and 0.001.
8w. **Folio reset mechanism (h46).** Three physical mechanisms for the folio break and one-edit chains were tested. At short lag (`6-20`), one-edit pair rates drop across folio boundaries in all three hands (1A effect +0.020 vs null 95th +0.008; 2B +0.024 vs +0.015; 3B +0.015 vs +0.011). At `21-100`, 1A (+0.006 vs +0.002) and 3B (+0.008 vs +0.007) pass the within-quire rotation null, while 2B (+0.006 vs +0.011) does not because within-quire rotation preserves the same clustered burst geometry (`F1` fails). Folio-opening first lines are *less* connected by one-edit mutations to the rest of their folio than interior lines are (`seed - interior` = −0.017, −0.024, −0.019 in 1A, 2B, 3B; `F2` fails), consistent with line-1 register specialization rather than first-line copy seeding. Across consecutive folios, one-edit rates across a visible open spread (`v -> r`) are no higher than across a page turn (`r -> v`) (`F3` fails).
8x. **Unified page-and-line generator (h47).** Adding partial lookback-buffer reset at folio boundaries (`rho_keep = 0.15`) and line-boundary slot framing (resetting the cross-word junction rule at line breaks, weighting paragraph-first and line-initial onsets and line-final endings) to the no-plaintext copier (`page_line_copier`) passes all 6 benchmark families simultaneously (`B1` 5/5 bifolium fingerprint, `B2` widened space band + junction $J \ge 0.06$, `B3` line-edge concentration + cross-line drop + para-initial gallows, `B4` `h43` folio-edge break, `B5` folio-concentrated burstiness, `B6` `h42` page-scale one-edit decay) in Hands 1A (6/6), 2B (6/6), and 3B (6/6; 5/6 if `d=80` unscored is excluded), whereas the stored `h39` baselines pass only 1/6 (`native_tuned`) and 2/6 (`C0_graph`). `U1`–`U3` pass.
8y. **Within-folio copy drift vs stationary palette (h48).** Restricted to line pairs on the *same* folio ($\ge 8$ lines) and tested against 200 within-folio line-order permutations (which hold each folio's exact word multiset and line lengths fixed), all three MS hands show strong top-to-bottom decay with line lag $\Delta\ell$ in both one-edit rate (`D1` passes 3/3: $S_{\text{edit}} = +0.0086, +0.0093, +0.0060$, $z = +5.2, +11.5, +7.8$) and unit-3gram cosine (`D2` passes 3/3: $S_{\text{cos3}} = +0.042, +0.042, +0.030$, $z = +10.5, +19.1, +14.0$). Prose controls (`it_boccaccio`, `la_pliny_herbal`) have $S_{\text{edit}} \le 0$. Decomposing short-lag ($\Delta\ell \in \{1,2\}$) same-folio one-edit excess over a within-quire shuffle (`D3` passes 3/3) shows that both a folio-wide vocabulary shift (37–63 %, $z = 5.5\text{–}11.7$) and sequential line-to-line drift down the page (37–63 %, $z = 5.2\text{–}10.9$) contribute in every hand.
9. **Open:** whether the text is language, cipher, process-generated text, or a mixture; what makes the suffix-light/prefix-heavy shape; what carries the *size* of the section difference; why every model underpredicts how much new vocabulary a new section brings (h07); whether anything survives genuinely medieval vernacular controls.
10. **Biggest caveats:** generators are fitted to the same manuscript they are tested on (circular for any matched metric), most earlier folds are interleaved by folio and earlier CIs used 8-page blocks (quire SEs are a median 1.67× larger, h08), sections and quires coincide, control families have 3–13 independent base texts, and the third-party transcription/images carry no open licence (see §5).

### Synthesis of the word-boundary loop (h33–h39)

* **Spaces are real and rule-bound (h33).** Moving or joining spaces never makes more word-like units, in MS or in
  Latin/Italian; boundary positions are predictable from two glyphs either side (S ≈ .72–.80 per hand), between
  natural text and the generators.
* **The end→start word link is a gap rule, not hidden words (h31, h33, h34).** A per-hand rule choosing the next word
  by its first glyph given the previous last glyph reproduces the link (r .90–.97 with MS's table) and keeps the 5/5
  fingerprint, with Italian or nonsense input alike.
* **Space placement comes from word shape (h35).** Re-spelling generator words with each hand's glyph-shape model puts
  spaces in range; `l`, `y`, `r` as word-final glyphs carry most of the gap.
* **But shape and copying pull against each other (h35–h39).** Whole-word re-spelling fixes spaces and destroys the
  near-copy network the fingerprint needs; glyph-level maps keep copies but over-regularise spaces; tree- and
  family-wise schemes (h37, h38) keep part of each. The best compromise (h39 A, one annealed substitution per slot)
  reaches near-copy .74–.83, 5/5 in 4/9 cells and spaces .74–.84, never all targets at once.
* **MS's local word similarity is not literal copying at face value (h38, h39 B).** A copier tuned to MS's near-repeat
  rate overshoots the 5-word copy statistic 5–7× and leaves spaces far too unpredictable.
* **Plaintext never matters.** At every step Italian (Boccaccio) and Zipf nonsense give the same results, so none of
  these fits is evidence for or against an underlying language, and none supports any reading.

## 3. What each result does and does not show

* **Letter-level entropy (rows 3–4).** Shows: with the stated normalisation and unit segmentation, MS 408's conditional letter entropy is lower than any plain natural-language, abjad or simple-cipher control tested, including after alphabet-size adjustment. Does not show: that the text is *not* language (a transformation of language, or a language with very different orthography, is not excluded); abjad + verbose substitution is only crudely simulated; only 17 base texts, no genuine medieval vernacular herbal.
* **Position-bound words, suffix-light affixes (row 5).** Shows: a strong, measurable word-internal regularity; a pure independent-slot model describes it worse than a within-word bigram, whereas a slot-*conditioned* bigram is the best held-out description tested. Does not show: that slots are syllables, morphemes or anything linguistic (h03 finds the syllable reading non-diagnostic); affix learning is heuristic and depends on the unit definition.
* **Mechanisms (rows 6–7).** Shows: which published mechanisms get which metrics; the copy-and-modify layer (self-citation-like local repetition) supplies adjacent repeats and extra rare types that the cipher lacks, and a hybrid generalises to held-out folios. Does not show: that MS 408 was made by either mechanism. A fitted generator is an existence result, the copy layer rather than the plaintext produces the gain, and every generator misses the suffix rate.
* **Section signal (rows 8–9, 15–16).** Shows: section vocabulary differs beyond Currier A/B, and can be produced without plaintext when parameters track the section. With quire-level CIs (h08), neither "beyond drift" nor "JS above the best no-plaintext generator" is significant any more, and sections and quires nearly coincide, so this may be a quire (scribe, time, batch) effect as much as a topic effect. h10 measures it directly: with quire and hand held fixed, the section difference shrinks to roughly a quarter to a third of the hand/language difference within a section, and too few pages remain to confirm it against drift. In h07, no model predicts an unseen section's vocabulary from the others, and every model underpredicts its novelty. Does not show: that it is topic-driven, scribal, or procedural; the data cannot tell them apart.
* **Drawings (row 17).** Shows: picture-based word meanings cannot be separated from section/quire membership with this manuscript's layout. Labels on the same object type share endings a little more than chance. Does not show any word meaning; one fragile root-word transfer is a lead for a properly powered, independently tagged replication, not a reading.
* **Tone sheet and tone maps (rows 10–12).** Shows: the sheet is not a translation table (coverage, occurrence, section tests) and its Hz values contain no information about how to extend them; every positional pitch map is circular. Does not show who made the sheet, and a pitch assignment is a hypothesis-generation device, not evidence about the manuscript.
* **Zodiac (rows 13–14).** Shows: the zodiac centres hold no Voynichese sign names to crib from; labels differ page to page with the section's drift; label `o-` and q absence are zodiac features that generators fitted on other text do not reproduce; ring text is not unusually page-local; calendar adjacency is indistinguishable from page order. Does not show that labels are or are not names: it shows that the specific name-list, affix and crib tests found nothing beyond the nulls.

## 4. Major caveats

* **Circular fitting.** Hybrid, section-varying and zodiac-fitted generators are fitted on MS 408 and scored on MS 408-derived metrics; the grammar behind several (~1.4–1.6k parameters), the Java self-citation generator (design embeds MS-derived glyph classes and a seed line) and the slot-grammar control (written with Voynichese in mind) all carry MS-derived structure. A match shows a metric does not discriminate, not that the mechanism is right. Per-section seed pools memorise vocabulary by construction; parameter counts are reported, and the AIC-like comparisons are descriptive rankings, not likelihoods. Position-based pitch maps (h02/h03) are circular for the contour statistics they were built to produce.
* **Interleaved folds.** Hybrid and section tests use 3 folds with folio *i* → fold *i* mod 3, so neighbouring folios of the same section, hand and quire sit in training. This tests generalisation across folios within sections, not across sections or quires; there is no folio→quire map, and "quire-level" CIs use 8-folio blocks as a stand-in. h03 uses folio-parity folds (same caveat).
* **Small, simplified controls.** 3–13 independent base texts per family (windows from one text are correlated), Macer and Middle High German undersized and excluded from verdicts, Hebrew/Arabic = one language family in two scripts, no Syriac/Ethiopic/Greek/Occitan/Old French/Czech, no Dioscorides or Pseudo-Apuleius, "herbal Latin" is a topic control not a genre match; most transformations (verbose, Naibbe-*inspired*, nomenclator, self-citation-style) are crude; the faithful Naibbe and Timm–Schinner implementations were evaluated separately (stage 6). The Cryptologia and self-citation papers themselves were not readable (bot wall / paywall), only the authors' code, tables and excerpts; Zattera's probabilities were unavailable.
* **Measurement choices.** A transcription is a model (token = blank-delimited chunk, uncertain spaces treated as spaces by default); Currier A/B comes from the transcription's page variable; unit definitions differ between MS 408 (collapsed EVA units) and controls (characters); SEs capture sampling noise, not transcription or segmentation uncertainty; pages and lines are not independent. Most later stages (word structure, mechanisms, hybrids, h06) use ZL 3b only; replication on IT 2a / RF 1b was done for the basic statistics.
* **Image stage.** 28 text pages, proxy metrics, parameters tuned on the same pages; no ground-truth boxes; herbal, zodiac and fold-out pages not handled.
* **Zodiac statistics.** 12 pages, 934 ring + 336 label tokens: wide SEs, verdict counts move by ±2 with seeds; several Roman month readings are disputed; label order is locus order.
* **Multiple testing.** Many metrics are correlated; the pre-specified Holm corrections are noted where used (h01, h02, h03, h06), but most other `≈` counts are screens used to order columns, not hypothesis tests.
* **Licensing of data and images.** The voynich.nu transcription files carry no stated open licence, so they are **not committed** (only `MANIFEST.json` with URLs and SHA256, and the fetch code; `python -m msdecipher fetch` recreates them). Control texts are git-ignored, fetched from public-domain / CC sources (Perseus CC BY-SA 4.0, Wikisource, Gutenberg, Sefaria, Tanzil — the Quran text is CC BY 3.0 "verbatim copies only", so it is kept unmodified and normalised in memory only). Vendored third-party data: the Naibbe tables (modified MIT: keep the copyright notice and cite Greshko 2025), Stolfi's word grammar (rules only, with attribution), and texts generated by Timm & Schinner's MIT-licensed Java generator ([data/mechanisms/README.md](data/mechanisms/README.md)). **Yale page images:** the PDF is stored in `source/` as two halves and a few annotated excerpts / glyph crops from the scans are committed under `reports/image_stage/` and `glyphs/image/`; the item's usage-rights statement could not be read by the automated fetcher (HTTP 403), so confirm Yale's terms before redistributing any image-derived file; rendered full-page images are git-ignored.

## 5. Prioritised next steps

1. **Model hand/language explicitly (started in h11).** The per-hand generator is done (h12): it works, but only through its word grammar. Next: a copy objective that can identify copying at hand-sized samples (e.g. adjacent-edit and recurrence statistics weighted up, or likelihood-based fitting), and a unit-alignment orthography model on the full vocabulary, not just top words, to measure how much of the hand gap is spelling. h10 shows hand/language is the largest factor. Any section, topic or picture-word claim should be tested within hand (or with hand as a covariate), and generators should be fitted per hand. The non-confounded section contrasts (Q15, Q9, Q10, Q14) are too small for drift-aware nulls. Gaining power would need finer units (lines or paragraphs) with a defensible drift null.
2. **Explain vocabulary novelty and the suffix rate.** Every model underpredicts how much new vocabulary an unseen section brings (h07), and all miss the suffix-light shape (21 %). Test heavier-tailed, section-specific vocabularies and affix-aware innovation.
3. **Replicate the h09 root-word lead independently.** Have a second annotator tag herbal root size blind, on higher-resolution scans, then re-run the pre-registered transfer test with fixed k. Resolve the pharmaceutical `[row,col]` references to single labels for the same-plant test.
4. **Better controls.** Medieval vernacular herbals/Dioscorides/Pseudo-Apuleius, Syriac/Ethiopic, Occitan/Old French/Czech; more independent base texts per family; group-level units for verbose ciphers.
5. **Replicate the later stages on IT 2a, RF 1b (and GC 2a once its alphabet is mapped)** to separate transcription artefacts from structure. Use quire-level CIs (`msdecipher.quires`) and bifolium-safe folds by default.
6. **Higher-resolution image work** (herbal and zodiac pages, per-hand comparison, ground-truth boxes on a small hand-annotated set).
7. **Pre-register the validation gates** (WORKFLOW stage 12) before any new candidate-plaintext attack, as h07 and h09 did.
8. Only then: syntax induction and candidate-plaintext/crib attacks. Nothing in this repo licenses skipping steps 1–7.
