# Word structure (Workflow Stages 6 and 7): slot grammar, affixes, behavioural clusters

> **MS 408 is undeciphered.** This stage measures *structure*: how well a positional grammar compresses and predicts the vocabulary, and whether frequent words fall into stable groups by where they occur. Slots are numbered positions and clusters are numbered groups. **No cluster is a verb, noun or name**; "behavioural tags" are descriptive thresholds (e.g. line-initial rate > 1.5x baseline). A stable grouping is evidence of distributional regularity, not of syntax or meaning.

Run: `python -m msdecipher word-structure` (about 10-15 min on 8 cores; `--workers`, `--k`, `--no-jackknife`, `--no-sweep`, `--reuse` to rebuild verdicts/report/plots from the CSVs already in `reports/word_structure/`). Outputs: `REPORT.md`, `comparison.csv` (all metrics, every corpus), `ms408_ci.csv`, `ms408_jackknife_replicates.csv`, `verdicts.csv`, `verdict_summary.csv`, `slot_count_sweep.csv`, `ms408_slot_*.csv`, `ms408_affixes.csv`, `ms408_clusters_*.csv`, `figures/`.

## Stage 6: intra-word structure (`slot_grammar.py`, `word_structure.py`)
- **Slot grammar** (in the spirit of Stolfi's word grammar and Zattera's slot table, but *learned*, not hand-written): K ordered slots; each slot is skipped or emits one unit from its own distribution; a word is the sequence of emissions of the non-skipped slots (a skip-HMM, fitted by EM with restarts, light pruning, slots with emission probability < 0.005 treated as inactive). Fitted separately at **type level** (each distinct word once; the vocabulary) and **token level** (frequency-weighted). K = 12 everywhere; a sweep over K = 4..14 is in `slot_count_sweep.csv`.
- **Baselines:** unit unigram, within-word unit bigram (with boundary symbols), a token lexicon with escape to the bigram, and a **prefix-core-suffix** model (`AffixModel`: affixes ranked by productivity, hard-EM segmentation, bigram core model).
- **Held-out generalisation:** 4-fold cross-validation by block (folio for MS 408, 500-token chunk for controls). Reported: bits/type, bits/token, bits per *new* type (types unseen in training), type/token coverage of the pruned grammar, share of new types it can generate.
- **Compression / MDL:** description length = held-out data bits + 0.5·log2(N) per used parameter, slot vs bigram vs unigram (`mdl_*`).
- **Null:** the same models on a copy of the text with units shuffled *inside* each word (keeps word lengths and unit counts, destroys position). `slot_null_gap` = null bits − real bits: how much of the grammar's compression depends on position.
- Words longer than K units are dropped (share reported as `share_over_K`).

## Stage 7: behavioural word clusters (`word_classes.py`)
- Top 200 word types. Features: PPMI-weighted left/right neighbour profiles over the top 200 words (+ an "other" and a boundary context), reduced by truncated SVD. Three feature sets: **neighbour** (flat running text; the only one run on controls), **line** (adds line-boundary contexts, line-initial/final rates), **full** (adds section, Currier A/B and hand distributions; MS 408 only).
- Clustering: k-means (k = 5 for the cross-corpus comparison; best k by silhouette also reported), Ward agglomerative and HDBSCAN as cross-checks.
- **Stability:** ARI between the full-data clustering and clusterings of block-bootstrap resamples (`k5_ari_boot`); ARI between clusterings of random half-splits of blocks (`k5_ari_split`); silhouette. **Quality / structure:** held-out adjacent-class mutual information minus its shuffled-text value (`class_mi_excess`), NMI of clusters with the last / first unit of the words (minus a permutation baseline), NMI with section and Currier A/B (MS 408).
- Reference runs on MS 408 with tokens shuffled (kills neighbour structure) and units shuffled inside words (kills word-internal structure).

## Controls and CIs
Identical analyses on every row of `reports/controls/` (same 35k-token size-matched samples): Latin (incl. herbal/medical), Italian, German, Hebrew/Arabic, vowel-removed abjad, substitution/polyalphabetic/nomenclator/verbose/Naibbe-like ciphers, shuffles, reversal, alphagram, Markov/iid gibberish, slot-grammar and self-citation generators (158 control rows; stochastic ones: seed 1). MS 408 CIs: delete-one-block jackknife over 8-page blocks (26 replicates; `ms408_ci.csv`; estimate ± 1.96 SE; see CONTROLS.md for why not a with-replacement bootstrap). Family verdicts: 95% prediction interval including MS 408's SE, as in the controls stage.

## What the run shows (ZL 3b, collapsed units; numbers from `reports/word_structure/REPORT.md`)
1. **Position carries unusually much of MS 408's word structure.** Shuffling units inside words makes the slot grammar 2.93 bits/type worse (95% CI 2.66-3.19) versus a median of 0.60 and a maximum of 1.47 across the 31 natural-language/abjad rows; MS 408 is above all 31 (z ≈ +5 against natural plaintext, +7 against Hebrew/Arabic). Only the alphagram control (sorted letters, 4.3-7.8) and the slot-grammar generator (4.6-5.8; written with Voynichese in mind) are higher; polyalphabetic and verbose controls have negative gaps.
2. **But a K=12 slot grammar is *not* a good description by itself.** It recovers 68% (CI 65-71%) of the unigram→bigram gain at type level (natural median 70%, so not distinctive), and is 2.57 bits/type *worse* than the within-word bigram (natural plaintext 1.79 ± 0.40; Latin 1.63; Hebrew/Arabic −0.49: there the slot grammar wins). MDL, which charges 178 slot parameters against 644 bigram parameters, still prefers the bigram by 2.27 bits/type (CI 1.97-2.57; natural median 1.50, 27 of 31 below MS 408). So the positional skeleton is real, but there is also dependence between *adjacent* units within a slot-defined word that independent slot emissions cannot capture (e.g. multi-unit chunks like `qo`, `edy`, `aiin`). Conclusion: "slots + something more" is needed; a pure K-slot model is not it. A richer or slot-conditioned model, or Zattera's table, was not tested.
3. **Generalisation and coverage are not discriminating:** held-out type coverage 98.3% (bigram baseline 98.4%), and 47% of held-out types were never seen in training (both models generate 96.5% of them); coverage is saturated by permissive pruning, bits are the sharper measure. Bits for new types follow the same pattern as seen types (slot − bigram 2.58, CI 2.17-2.98).
4. **Prefix-core-suffix:** 39 learned prefixes, 34 suffixes; 82% of types have a prefix (inside the natural range, 0.70 ± 0.19) but only 21% a suffix versus 0.67-0.73 in natural text (z ≤ −5 against every natural family): MS 408 words look prefix-heavy and suffix-light under this model. The affix model is 0.13 bits/type worse than the bigram (CI 0.00-0.25). Affix learning is heuristic (top-productivity candidates, hard EM) and depends on unit definition (Latin/Italian suffixes are inflections, MS 408 has none identified), so treat this as descriptive.
5. **Behavioural clusters are moderately stable and no more structured than natural text.** k=5 on neighbour features: ARI vs block-bootstrap 0.42 (CI 0.12-0.73), half-split ARI 0.20, silhouette 0.12 versus natural-language plaintext 0.43 ± 0.11, 0.28 ± 0.12, 0.14 ± 0.02. With tokens shuffled the same pipeline gives 0.05 / 0.03 / 0.06, so the clusters are real regularities of word order, but of about the strength seen in Latin, Italian, Hebrew and Arabic; adjacent-class MI excess 0.12 bits (CI 0.01-0.23) is *below* the natural median 0.17. The `line` (3-5 clusters, line-final / line-initial-leaning groups) and `full` (section / Currier A/B / hand; NMI with Currier language 0.33, with section 0.31) variants add describable tendencies, e.g. a group of 8 words with 36% line-initial rate; these cannot be compared with controls, which lack line and section metadata. Currier A and B alone are too small (11k / 23k tokens) for stable clusters (ARI 0.09 / 0.28).
6. **First-unit link:** clusters correlate with the first unit of the word more than in natural text (NMI excess 0.27 vs 0.06 ± 0.04), and with the last unit about as in natural text (0.12 vs 0.08 ± 0.03), but the jackknife CIs on both (±0.26, ±0.22) overlap the natural values: **not distinguishable** at this sample size.
7. **Families:** the number of the 42 metrics for which MS 408 is inside the prediction interval is highest for word reversal / alphagram / shuffled tokens / self-citation (30-35) and lowest for Latin-only, herbal/medical Latin and vowel-removed abjad (19); this ordering is not a likelihood (word-level metrics, which these families preserve from plaintext, dominate the count). The discriminating metrics are the within-word ones above, as in the controls stage (h2, shuffle gap).
8. **Slot count (K=4..14):** the slot−bigram gap on MS 408 is 0.2 bits at K=4 (but 49% of types are dropped as too long), 2.1 at K=6, peaks at 2.8 at K=8 (when nearly all types are included) and drifts down to 2.5 at K=14; K=12 is not the cause of the poor fit, but more slots do not close the gap either. Absolute bits are not comparable across K.

## Caveats
- One positional model (independent emissions per slot) and one clustering recipe; results depend on K, the top-200 cutoff, SVD dimension and k = 5.
- Units differ between MS 408 (collapsed EVA: ch, sh, cth, ckh, cph, cfh) and controls (characters); ratios and gaps are less sensitive than absolute bits. The raw-EVA row (gap 3.86 slot−bigram) shows how much the unit choice matters.
- Folds are blocks of one text: held-out numbers say nothing about unseen sections and are optimistic for within-section generalisation.
- MS 408 SEs are a block jackknife (sampling noise only; no quire map); control rows are single samples (stochastic: seed 1), so family intervals are optimistic. Some ratios (`slot gain frac`) blow up when unigram ≈ bigram (iid letters: 2.5-4.1).
- The `slot-grammar generator` control was written with Voynichese's slot structure in mind; its match is expected, not independent evidence.
- Not done: Zattera/Stolfi exact tables as baselines, slot-conditioned bigrams, Brown clustering, cluster-level syntactic tests, per-hand/section controls, quire-level CIs.
