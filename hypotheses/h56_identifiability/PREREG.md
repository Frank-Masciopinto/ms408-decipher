# h56: is a semantic layer identifiable after the copying routine? (pre-registration)

Written before any h56 computing. No translation is claimed. Do not edit this file after it is committed.

## Question
The copying routine accounts for the dominant statistics of the running text. A readable layer is identifiable only if some residual of the transcription still predicts an external variable, or a correction, after hand, quire, folio palette, and local copying are removed. This file fixes seven stages. Stage 7 runs only if an earlier stage produces a grounded association.

Pixel resegmentation is not treated as a posterior. The repository's image stage did not recover the transcription's units. Stage 1 uses the transcriber's own reading lattice and a second EVA transcription.

## Data
- Primary file: `data/transcriptions/ZL3b-n.txt`, parsed by `msdecipher.ivtff`.
- Second EVA transcription: `IT2a-n.txt`, same parser.
- Hands `1A`, `2B`, `3B` from `data/quires.csv` (`lfd_hand` + `currier_language`).
- Visual tags: `hypotheses/h09_image_context_cribs/data/visual_tags.csv`.
- Tags used everywhere below: `jars`, `roots`, `nymphs`, `stars`. A folio is positive for a tag when its value is greater than 0.
- Edit distance is character Levenshtein distance on the stored EVA word. Distance 1 means exactly one insertion, deletion, or substitution. No other distance is used.

## Stage 1 — reading lattice
Three readings of ZL paragraph text, plus IT2a paragraph text as a fourth:

1. `primary`: the parser's default reading.
2. `alternate`: if a token's first alternative group has at least two options, substitute the second option for the first inside the token; otherwise keep the token.
3. `joined`: uncertain spaces (commas) join tokens.
4. `it2a`: IT2a primary reading.

On each reading and each hand, using folio order:

- **G**, bigram gain. Train on even folios, test on odd folios. With add-0.5 smoothing, gain is the mean over test pairs of `log2 p(w_t | w_{t-1}) − log2 p(w_t)`. Pairs do not cross folios.
- **E**, adjacent character-edit-distance-1 rate, same folio, successive tokens.
- **O**, line-initial special-onset rate minus the same rate off the line start. A special onset is a word whose collapsed EVA units (`ch, sh, cth, ckh, cph, cfh`) begin with `d`, `s`, `p`, `t`, `k`, or `f`.

`U1` passes when, on all four readings and all three hands, `G < 0`, `E > 0.03`, and `O > 0`.

Report the fraction of ZL paragraph tokens that carry an alternative group. No image clustering is scored.

## Stage 2 — procedural surprise
For each hand, even quires estimate:

- `P_hand`, the unigram;
- `P_pos`, the unigram inside line position `init`, `mid`, or `final`;
- the edit-1 neighborhood of every type that occurs at least 3 times.

Odd quires are scored, then the split is reversed and the two test sets are pooled. For a token `w` with previous token `prev` on the same line:

`p(w) = 0.40 P_folio + 0.20 P_hand + 0.25 P_pos + 0.15 P_local`

`P_folio` is the unigram of the other lines on the same folio. `P_local` puts mass 1/2 on `prev` when `prev` is in the training vocabulary and mass 1/2 uniformly on the training edit-1 neighbors of `prev`. If that support is empty, `P_local = P_hand`. Every distribution is add-0.5 over the training vocabulary plus `OTHER`. Surprise is `-log2 p(w)`.

`R1`, labels against the routine. Score each label token with `0.5 P_folio_paragraph + 0.5 P_hand` (no local term). Compare its mean surprise with paragraph tokens on the same folio whose character length differs by at most 1. The folio contrast is the label mean minus the matched paragraph mean, then averaged over folios that have both. The null, 2000 times, seed 56, permutes label-token lists across those folios inside each quire and rescores the moved labels against the destination folio's paragraph unigram. `R1` passes when the observed mean contrast is positive and the one-sided permutation `p < 0.05`.

`R2`, illustrated pages. For each of the four tags, compare mean paragraph-token surprise on positive folios with negative folios. The null permutes the tag inside the quire, 2000 times, seed 56. Holm correction across the four tags. `R2` passes when at least one tag has higher surprise on positive folios and Holm `p < 0.05`.

## Stage 3 — folio roots
Inside each folio, connect two paragraph types when their character edit distance is 1. In each component, the root is the type with the highest token count; ties keep the type that occurs earlier. A component is kept when it contains at least 3 tokens.

Coverage is the fraction of paragraph tokens whose type is in a kept component. It is reported and is not a pass rule.

`M1`. Keep roots that are the root of at least 8 folios. For each kept root and each of the four tags, the statistic is the root's rate on positive folios minus its rate on negative folios. The null permutes the tag inside the quire, 2000 times, seed 56. Holm correction across all root–tag pairs. `M1` passes when a pair has a positive difference and Holm `p < 0.05`.

## Stage 4 — labels and drawings
Only folios that have at least one label and a visual-tag row.

`L1`, counts. Spearman correlation between the number of label tokens and each of `jars`, `roots`, `nymphs`, `stars`. The null permutes the tag inside the quire, 2000 times, seed 56. Holm across four tags. A tag passes when the correlation is positive and Holm `p < 0.05`.

`L2`, label onsets. Collapsed first unit of each label word of length at least 2. For each tag, Jensen–Shannon divergence (log base 2) between label onsets on positive and negative folios. The null permutes the tag inside the quire among label-bearing folios, 2000 times, seed 56. Holm across four tags. A tag passes when the divergence exceeds the null 95th percentile and Holm `p < 0.05`.

`L1` or `L2` passes when any tag passes.

## Stage 5 — recorded uncertainty
Multispectral images are not in the repository. No erased-stroke recovery is claimed. The recorded lattice is the alternative groups, unreadable markers, and ligatures in ZL.

`C1` passes when at least 80% of alternative groups have both the first and second option of length at most 3. That is glyph-scale uncertainty. The stage cannot recover erasures that were never encoded.

## Stage 6 — synthetic recovery
The detector is the stage-3 rule: the most frequent type in each folio is its top root.

Two synthetic manuscripts, each 40 folios, 8 lines, 8 words, vocabulary of 40 strings, numpy seed 56:

- `null_stream`: with probability 0.7 copy a token already used on the folio, otherwise draw uniformly; then with probability 0.5 apply one random character substitution.
- `planted_stream`: the same process, except each folio has a topic in `{0,1,2,3}` and 12 of the 40 strings are topic markers (3 each). With probability 0.25 a token is replaced by a uniform draw from that folio's three markers.

Recovery accuracy is the fraction of folios whose top root is one of that folio's three markers. The null stream is scored against a topic assignment it does not use, so its expected accuracy is the rate at which a frequency root happens to be one of a random topic's three markers.

`S6` passes when planted accuracy is at least 0.50 and null-stream accuracy is at most 0.25.

If `S6` fails, a negative result in stages 2–4 is `inconclusive`, because the detector was not shown to see a planted signal.

## Stage 7 — gate
A grounded association is `R1`, `R2`, `M1`, or stage 4.

- If `S6` fails, stage 7 is `inconclusive` and no reading is proposed.
- If `S6` passes and there is no grounded association, stage 7 is `unidentifiable` and no reading is proposed.
- If `S6` passes and there is a grounded association, recompute that association on odd quires only. It is `confirmed` when the held-out sign matches the full-sample sign and the held-out one-sided `p < 0.10`. Otherwise it is `unconfirmed`. No mapping from tokens to natural-language words is fit in either case.
