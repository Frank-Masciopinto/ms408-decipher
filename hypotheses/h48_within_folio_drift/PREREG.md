# h48: within-folio copy drift vs fixed page palette (pre-registration)

Written before any h48 computing. No translation claims. Do not edit this file after it is committed.

## Question
`h43` and `h44` narrowed the manuscript's page-level clustering to two distinct mechanisms inside each folio:
- **Mechanism A (top-to-bottom copy drift):** The scribe generates text sequentially down the folio, glancing back a few lines and mutating recent words. Under this mechanism, similarity and one-edit links between lines on the *same* folio decay with line distance $\Delta \ell = |\ell_2 - \ell_1|$, and shuffling line order within each folio destroys the short-line-lag excess.
- **Mechanism B (stationary page palette / schematic table):** Each folio has a fixed local vocabulary palette (set by the page's topic, diagram, or plant) from which lines are drawn in any order. Under this mechanism, two lines on the same folio are equally similar whether they are 1 line apart or 10 lines apart.

Does within-folio similarity in MS 408 decay with line distance inside a single page, or is a folio's vocabulary stationary from top to bottom?

## Data
Paragraph lines in reading order from `h30_lib.load_ms(hand)` (`h14_lib.table()`, ZL3b `ZL3b-n.txt`, comma as a word space) for hands `1A`, `2B`, `3B`, restricted to folios with at least 8 paragraph lines.
Controls and comparison streams (segmented into fake folios of 25 lines of ~9 words where real folios are absent):
- **Prose controls:** `it_boccaccio`, `la_pliny_herbal` (using real chapter stretches from `h43` with $\ge 8$ lines of 9 tokens).
- **Drift generators:** `self_citation` (`fitted_s1.txt`), `h39_native_tuned` (`lines_2B_native_tuned.json`), and a synthetic **stationary-page-palette control** (`palette_ctrl`: each folio permutes its own lines randomly, preserving the exact folio word multiset and line lengths while erasing within-folio drift).

## Measures (computed strictly on line pairs `(l_1, l_2)` on the SAME folio)
Line lag $\Delta \ell = \ell_2 - \ell_1 \in \{1, 2, 3\text{–}4, 5\text{–}8, 9+\}$ (where $\ell_1, \ell_2$ are 0-based paragraph-line indices on that folio).
1. **M1 — Within-folio line-lag one-edit rate (`edit1_lag`):** For every pair of words `(w_a in l_1, w_b in l_2)` on the same folio with `|len(u_a) - len(u_b)| <= 1`, compute the one-edit rate `P(edit1 | Delta_l)` in each line-lag bin.
   - **Drift slope (`S_edit`):** `P(edit1 | Delta_l in {1, 2}) - P(edit1 | Delta_l >= 5)`.
2. **M2 — Within-folio line-lag lexical overlap (`jaccard_lag`):** Mean word-type Jaccard similarity (and unit-3gram cosine similarity `cos3_lag`) between line $\ell_1$ and line $\ell_2$ on the same folio as a function of $\Delta \ell$.
   - **Drift slope (`S_cos3`):** `mean_cos3(Delta_l in {1, 2}) - mean_cos3(Delta_l >= 5)`.
3. **M3 — First-half vs second-half folio drift (`half_js`):** Split each folio (with $\ge 8$ lines) into top half (first $\lfloor L/2 \rfloor$ lines) and bottom half (last $\lfloor L/2 \rfloor$ lines). Compute the mean cosine similarity between top-half and bottom-half word-count vectors, minus the mean under 200 within-folio line-order permutations (where a negative observed-minus-null value means the top and bottom halves of a folio have drifted apart more than random line assignment on the same folio).

## Null (Stationary Page Palette Null)
Permute the order of lines **within each folio** 200 times (numpy seed 48). Because line lengths and the exact set of words on each folio are invariant under within-folio line permutation, any folio-level topic or fixed page palette produces zero excess under this null. A positive `S_edit` or `S_cos3` above the 95th percentile of the 200 within-folio line permutations proves **within-folio sequential drift** beyond a fixed page lexicon.

## Predictions
- **D1 (within-folio one-edit drift):** At least two of the three MS hands (`1A`, `2B`, `3B`) have `S_edit` (`Delta_l in {1,2}` minus `Delta_l >= 5`) above the 95th percentile of the within-folio line-permutation null.
- **D2 (within-folio unit-3gram drift):** At least two of the three MS hands have `S_cos3` above the 95th percentile of the within-folio line-permutation null.
- **D3 (magnitude — drift vs palette share):** Decompose total same-folio one-edit excess over a quire-level shuffle into (a) the **folio-palette component** (same-folio line-permuted rate minus quire-shuffle rate) and (b) the **within-folio drift component** (`Delta_l in {1,2}` observed rate minus same-folio line-permuted rate). In at least two MS hands, **both** components are strictly positive ($> 2$ null SDs), showing that a folio has both a page-level vocabulary seed *and* sequential line-to-line drift down the page.
