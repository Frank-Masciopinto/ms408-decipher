# h54: are the h52 and h53 residuals adjacency and paragraph role, or copying artefacts? (pre-registration)

Written before any h54 computing. No translation claims. Do not edit this file after it is committed.

h52 and h53 are already computed. Their numbers are not inputs to these pass rules. Lag-2 ratios from h52 are already known and are not used as a pass rule here.

## Question
Two leftovers survived the page-and-line copier.

- h52 F2: the second unit of a word predicts the second unit of the next word after the touching junction is held fixed (about +0.02 bits).
- h53: the interior of a paragraph's first line differs from the interior of its last line.

Each has a mundane account. F2 may be one-edit near-copies, or two words drawn from the same line's vocabulary with no adjacency rule. The paragraph contrast may be the folio's first line, a short closing line, or ordinary top-to-bottom drift. Do the residuals survive nulls that keep those accounts and break only the claimed rule?

## Streams
Hands `1A`, `2B`, `3B`, and `page_line_copier` for the same hands, on the `h47` template (`h47_lib.load_template`, up to 10,500 tokens). Copier words are `generated_lines` in `hypotheses/h47_page_line_generator/results/streams.json`, zipped onto that template. Line counts and line lengths must match.

Slots, bucketing, and F2 pairs are exactly as in h52: collapsed EVA units, `core` = the second unit or `SHORT`, a label below 30 tokens becomes `OTHER`, F2 is `I(core_t ; core_{t+1} | end_t, onset_{t+1})` on successive words of the same line, and only strata with at least 20 pairs enter the sum.

Paragraph interiors are exactly as in h53: drop the first and last word of a line, and a line with fewer than 4 words contributes nothing. Word classes are the 40 most frequent interior words in the lines used by that test, frozen, plus `OTHER`. Divergence is Jensen–Shannon, log base 2 (`h30_lib.js`).

A paragraph is a maximal run of lines. On these streams a new paragraph starts at `para_first` or at a folio change. The first paragraph-line of a folio is the **folio-first** line.

## Part A — F2

### A1, near-copies removed
Drop a pair when any of these holds:

- the two unit tuples are identical
- their unit Levenshtein distance is exactly 1 (`h42_lib.edit1`)
- both interiors `units[1:-1]` are non-empty and equal

Recompute F2 with the h52 null: 200 shuffles of the right-hand core inside each junction stratum, numpy seed 54, a new generator for this test. `excess = observed − mean(null)`, `z = excess / sd(null)` with `sd` the population standard deviation. If `sd < 1e-12`, `z = 0`.

A hand passes A1 when all of these hold: at least 500 pairs remain in the sum, `excess >= 0.01`, `z >= 2.58`, and `excess(MS) − excess(copier)`, both filtered the same way, is `>= 0.01`.

### A2, within-line core shuffle
Use every F2 pair. The null permutes **core labels inside each line**, leaving every onset and every end where it is. 200 permutations, numpy seed 54, its own generator. A line with fewer than 2 words is left as it is. Conditional MI uses the same 20-pair cutoff.

A hand passes A2 on the same four inequalities as A1, with this null's excess, and with at least 500 pairs.

### What A says
- `stands` if A1 and A2 each pass on at least two hands.
- `killed` if each of A1 and A2 passes on fewer than two hands.
- `partial` otherwise.

## Part B — paragraph role

Every B test uses 200 within-paragraph shuffles of line order, numpy seed 54, its own generator per test. The 95th percentile of 200 finite draws is the sorted value at index 189. A draw with an empty bag is non-finite. A hand is unscored for that test unless all 200 draws are finite and the stated token floors are met. Unscored hands do not pass.

The copier is scored on the same paragraphs and the same line indexes.

### B1, folio-first lines removed
Drop every paragraph whose first line is folio-first. Keep paragraphs with at least 3 lines. Statistic: h53 word-class JS of opening interiors against closing interiors. A hand is scored only if each side has at least 200 tokens.

A hand passes B1 when it is scored, the observed JS is strictly above the null 95th percentile, `excess >= 0.01`, and the excess exceeds the copier's excess on the same paragraphs by `>= 0.01`.

### B2, stub closings removed
Keep a paragraph of at least 3 lines only when its first line and its last line each have at least 6 words. Folio-first paragraphs stay in. Same JS, same pass inequalities, same 200-token floor.

### B3, distance-matched middles
Keep a paragraph only when all of these hold:

- at least 4 lines
- the first line is not folio-first
- the first line and the last line each have at least 6 words
- at least two middle lines have at least 6 words

The middle pair is the earliest and the latest of those long middle lines. Four bags: opening interior, closing interior, earlier-middle interior, later-middle interior. A hand is scored only if each bag has at least 150 tokens.

`D = JS(opening, closing) − JS(earlier middle, later middle)`, with one class map frozen on all four bags. The null shuffles line order inside the paragraph and rebuilds the four roles with the same length rules. `D` is scored like the other excesses: a hand passes when it is scored, `D` is strictly above the null 95th percentile, `D >= 0.01`, and `D` exceeds the copier's `D` by `>= 0.01`.

### What B says
- `stands` if B3 passes on at least two hands.
- `killed` if B3 passes on no hand.
- `partial` if B3 passes on exactly one hand.

B1 and B2 are reported. They do not change this label.

## Overall
- `both_stand` if A is `stands` and B is `stands`.
- `slots_only` if A is `stands` and B is not.
- `paragraphs_only` if B is `stands` and A is not.
- `both_killed` if A is `killed` and B is `killed`.
- `mixed` otherwise.

## Conditional follow-ups
These run only when the gate below is open. Their rules are fixed here. If the gate is closed, the follow-up is `not_run` and is not computed.

### C, held-out core gain (gate: A2 passes on at least two hands)
If A1 also passes on at least two hands, use the A1 pair filter. Otherwise use every F2 pair.

Folds are folios in order of first appearance, fold = index modulo 5. A pair takes the folio of its line. For each test fold, estimate `P(core_{t+1} | end_t, onset_{t+1})` and `P(core_{t+1} | end_t, onset_{t+1}, core_t)` on the training pairs with add-0.5 smoothing. The core alphabet is the training alphabet plus `OTHER`. A test pair whose junction stratum never occurs in training is skipped. The fold gain is the mean over the remaining test pairs of `log2 p1 − log2 p0`. The reported gain is the pair-weighted mean of the five folds.

The same gain is computed for `it_cucina` (`h30_lib.load_control("italian/pg33954.txt")`), with characters as units and that loader's block pages as the folds.

- `language_scale` if the Cucina gain is positive and at least two hands have a positive gain at least half the Cucina gain.
- `invalid_control` if the Cucina gain is not positive.
- `not_language_scale` otherwise.

### D, shared closing vocabulary (gate: B3 passes on at least two hands)
On the B3 lines, normalize a word that ends in `edy` by replacing that ending with `ol`, and pool counts. For each hand, and for each normalized type with at least 8 tokens in the opening bag plus the closing bag,

`log ratio = log((n_close + 0.5) / N_close) − log((n_open + 0.5) / N_open)`.

For each pair of hands, take the types that qualify in both. If fewer than 15 types remain, that pair is unscored. Otherwise compute the Spearman rank correlation of the two log-ratio vectors.

Null: 200 draws, numpy seed 54. Inside each B3 paragraph, exchange the opening line and the closing line with probability 1/2. Recompute the three correlations. A pair passes when it is scored, the correlation is `>= 0.30`, and the correlation is strictly above the null 95th percentile (index 189).

- `shared` if all three hand pairs pass.
- `not_shared` otherwise.
