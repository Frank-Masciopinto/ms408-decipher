# h03: "try a slot grammar": pitch tied to slot index, words as slot paths, and a slot = syllable-position test

Follows h02 (`../h02_full_tone_map/`), whose report recommended `a_slotgrammar_ext` as the map to carry forward.
Everything is held out where it can be (grammar fitted on one folio parity, applied to the other, then swapped), compared with controls
(Latin x2, Italian, German, Hebrew, Arabic, the repo's slot-grammar generator, Naibbe cipher outputs, self-citation outputs, letter-shuffled and word-shuffled MS 408),
and reported with 95% CIs.

> **A frequency assignment is a hypothesis-generation device, not evidence.** It only means something where it beats a null, and a map built from the data
> (here: slots learned from MS 408 itself) is partly circular. Nothing below shows that Voynichese "is" music or encodes tones.

## Verdict (short)

1. **Principled slot map: done, and its headline property is a tautology.** Pitch = f(slot index) (143 cents per slot, 12 slots = the sheet's 174 -> 433 Hz span; optional within-slot offset by unit frequency rank).
   Because a slot grammar places the units of a word in strictly increasing slots, **100% of decoded words are strictly rising melodies by construction** (tested). The "melodic contour" result of h02 is therefore not evidence of anything and is not used here.
2. **The sheet's 8 values do not pin down a slot map.** Spearman between the sheet's Hz and the digraphs' slot position: rho = **0.52** (learned slots, n = 8, Fisher 95% CI [-0.29, 0.90], exact permutation p two-sided 0.20, one-sided 0.098)
   and **0.81** (Zattera slots, only n = 6 placeable because `ri` and `ae` cannot be generated by Zattera's slot order; CI [0.00, 0.98], p two-sided 0.072, one-sided 0.036).
   Across the 3 variants x 2 grammars the best two-sided p is 0.044, which is 0.26 after Holm correction over the 6 tests. Direction is positive (sheet order loosely follows slot order), strength is not distinguishable from chance with 6-8 points.
3. **Slot-occupancy patterns ("word chords") form a moderate vocabulary** (632 pattern types for 35k tokens with learned slots, 16 types cover half the tokens, 99 cover 90%, entropy 6.4 bits, Zipf slope -1.17), between the repo's slot generator (42 types) and natural-language texts (820-1786 types).
   A word fills ~4.5 of 12 slots and skips ~4.9 slots inside its span.
4. **Beyond letters, slot patterns add almost nothing.** Against the best of four capacity-matched letter features, held-out gain of the *learned*-slot pattern is **worse** for next word, line position, Currier A/B and section (differences -0.03 to -0.07 bits/word, CIs exclude 0).
   Only the hand-coded **Zattera** pattern shows a small **positive** edge on hand/section: **+0.023 bits/word** [0.011, 0.034] for Currier A/B and **+0.015** [0.002, 0.029] for section (permutation null mean -0.002 to -0.017, max +0.002; the edge is real but tiny, and Zattera's table was itself designed while studying A/B differences, so it is not independent).
   For next-word prediction the MS slot patterns behave like the Naibbe / self-citation outputs, not like natural language (natural texts gain +0.03 to +0.17 bits over last-letter; MS 408 +0.006 learned, +0.018 Zattera; self-citation defaults +0.006 to +0.015).
5. **"Slot = syllable position": not supported, not refuted; the statistics are non-diagnostic.** Under a Sukhotin vowel-like split MS 408 words alternate C/V like Latin (excess switch rate 0.17 vs 0.19 / 0.22 Latin, 0.14 Italian, 0.13 German, 0.06 Hebrew, 0.06 Arabic) and have ~0.40 vowel-like runs per extra unit (Latin 0.35-0.36).
   But self-citation text (no syllables by construction) gives 0.17-0.20 and 0.32-0.37, and Naibbe output 0.15-0.16 and 0.37-0.40. The CV pattern across Zattera slots (`C V | C C C C | V | C V | C | V`) is a CV-chain template, not an onset-nucleus-coda cycle, and it does not distinguish a syllabic reading from non-syllabic generators.

Bottom line: the slot grammar is a good *description* of word shape (that was already known), makes a rising melody for free, and its patterns carry no more information about neighbours, line position or section than the letters they are made of, with the one small exception of Zattera's slots for hand / section. It supports no decipherment claim.

## 1. A principled slot-tied pitch assignment

Method (`h03_lib.py`):

* Grammar: the repo's **learned 12-slot skip grammar** (`SlotGrammar`, EM, units = collapsed EVA: `ch sh cth ckh cph cfh` single units) and **Zattera's hand-coded 12-slot alphabet** (`mech_grammars.SlotModel(mask=ZATTERA_SLOTS)`, probabilities by EM, structure fixed; words outside the table are uncovered).
* Decoding: Viterbi over the skip-HMM gives each word its most probable strictly increasing slot path (`SlotFit.decode_one`); fitted on **training folios only** (folio parity = fold), applied to the held-out folios, then folds swapped.
* Pitch maps: `slot_log`: Hz = 174 x 2^(slot index x 143.4 / 1200), i.e. 174 Hz (slot 1) ... 433 Hz (slot 12); `slot_rank`: same plus within-slot offset (0 to 0.8 of a slot step) by frequency rank of the unit inside its slot;
  `slot_sheetfit`: linear Hz = a + b x slot fitted to the sheet's 8 values (a = 189 Hz, b = 16.5 Hz/slot learned; a = 174, b = 17.9 Hz/slot Zattera, mean-slot variant).
* Full tables: `results/slot_unit_hz_table_learned.csv` (109 rows) and `slot_unit_hz_table_zattera.csv` (34 rows): every unit with count, modal slot, slot distribution, mean slot, Hz under the three maps, and the 8 sheet digraphs with their slot positions and the sheet's own Hz. Emission matrices: `slot_emission_matrix_*.csv`. Heat map: `figures/slot_unit_heatmap.png`.

## 2. Words as slot paths ("chords"): what is informative

Which slots are filled (`results/slot_fill_probability.csv`, held-out): learned slots 12: 0.95, 7: 0.87, 4: 0.73, 3: 0.46, 9: 0.41, 1: 0.30, 5: 0.28, 6: 0.20, 8: 0.16, 10: 0.04, 11: 0.03, 2: 0.03 (slots 2, 10, 11 are nearly dead). Zattera slots 11: 0.74, 9: 0.54, 2: 0.45, 4: 0.41, 12: 0.40, 7: 0.36, 5: 0.35, 1: 0.20, 10: 0.19, 8: 0.15, 3: 0.09, 6: 0.05.
Most common chords (slot sets): learned `4-7-12` (6.8% of tokens), `7-12`, `3-4-7-12`, `1-3-4-7-12`, `4-7-8-9-12`, `5-7-12`; Zattera `8-9-10-11` (the `daiin` family), `5-9-11`, `5-7-11-12`, `2-4-9-11`, `8-9-11` (`results/top_slot_patterns.csv`).

Pattern vocabulary (table above, columns "pattern types", "entropy", "types for 50%/90%", "Zipf slope"; `figures/pattern_zipf.png`):
MS 408 has 632 learned-slot patterns for 35k tokens (16 cover 50%, 99 cover 90%, 6.43 bits, Zipf slope -1.17, 31% of types are hapax). Natural languages are richer (Latin 1380 / 8.45 bits, Hebrew 1556, Arabic 1786) except Italian / German (835 / 820, 6.7-6.8 bits, slopes -1.12 / -1.13), which are close to MS. The slot generator is far poorer (42 types), Naibbe is similar (426-572 types), self-citation defaults 482-681 and fitted outputs 291-329. Shuffling word *order* changes nothing (the vocabulary is word-internal); shuffling letters inside words collapses it (67 types). So the vocabulary is a real property of the word shapes, but pattern counts like these do not separate MS 408 from Naibbe or self-citation output.

## 3. "Slot = syllable-like position"

Caveat: Sukhotin's algorithm itself maximises C/V alternation and its vowel-like set for MS 408 is `a c e h n o y` (single `c`, `h`, `n` are artefacts of segmentation, `i` is called consonant-like); read differences between texts, not absolute values.

* **CV alternation and nuclei count are Latin-like in MS 408** (excess 0.17, 0.40 nuclei per extra unit; Latin 0.19-0.22 and 0.35-0.36) and clearly above Hebrew / Arabic (0.06, abjads write no vowels, so this contrast is expected) and above shuffled letters (-0.004).
* **They are equally produced by non-syllabic mechanisms**: self-citation (a copy-and-mutate process with no syllables) 0.17-0.20 / 0.32-0.37, the slot generator 0.27, the Naibbe cipher output 0.15-0.16 / 0.37-0.40. A syllabic reading is *consistent* with MS 408 but is not singled out.
* **Slot-level CV structure**: Zattera slots 1-2 (`q s d | o y`) are C then V; slots 3-6 (`l r | t k p f | ch sh | cth ckh ...`) are four consecutive consonant-like slots; slot 7 (`e`) V; 8 (`s d`) C; 9 (`o a`) V; 10 (`i`) classed C by Sukhotin; 11 (`d l r m n`) C; 12 (`y`) V. That is a chain `CV · CCCC V · CV · C · V`, i.e. the onset/nucleus/coda picture would need slots 3-6 to be a very heavy onset cluster of the second syllable, and "coda" slots (8, 10, 11) are interleaved with nuclei (9, 12). Words like `qokeedy` read as CV-CV-CV; words like `chedy` as CCV-CV. The learned grammar gives a similar mixed profile (`C V C C C V V C C V C V`).
  Slot segregation of vowel-like vs consonant-like units is significant (learned: index 0.79 vs null mean 0.57, p = 0.025; Zattera 0.91 vs 0.71, p = 0.015) but weaker than in Latin (norm. excess 0.76 / 0.68 for the two Latin texts) and Italian (0.90) and about as strong as German (0.52) and in the range of the self-citation outputs (0.17-0.58) and Naibbe outputs (0.22-0.27); the slot generator is 1.0 by design.
* So the "slot-filler pair = CV syllable" decoding hypothesis is not supported beyond what any slot-structured generator produces, and it yields no readable syllabary (Sukhotin classes are unstable for rare units, there are no syllable boundaries in the transcription to test against, and the Naibbe output shows these statistics survive a one-to-many cipher of Latin).

## 5. Limitations

* The slot index is a latent variable of a model fitted to this very text: pitch tied to it inherits every artefact of the fit (learned slot membership is soft; EM restarts move the sheet correlation between 0.4 and 0.7). Zattera's table is hand-coded from the literature and was designed on MS 408 itself.
* Only 6-8 sheet digraphs and three of them are frequent; `ri` (14), `ae` (4), `ya` (16), `ny` (13) rest on a handful of occurrences. The sheet correlation is therefore weak evidence either way.
* Held-out tests use folio parity (2 folds): neighbouring folios of the same hand and section land in both halves, which favours any feature that tracks hand or section. Controls without folio structure (Naibbe, generator) use 500-word blocks, and have no A/B / section labels (those tasks are MS-only, with a label-permutation null).
* The letter comparators are four of many possible; the Zattera edge could change with other comparators. The `uncovered` class for Zattera (18% of tokens) is part of its feature (the coverage flag alone gives +0.003 / +0.005 bits).
* Sukhotin classes are a crude proxy for vowel/consonant, depend on the unit inventory and are unstable for rare units; abjad texts have no vowels to find.
* Pseudo-lines of 8 words are used for controls without real lines; next-word tests only pair words within a line.
* Everything is on one transcription (ZL3b-n, readable tokens of the "P" generic locus type). Results can shift with transcription choice and with merging `ch/sh` and benched gallows into units.
