# h49: does stripping same-folio one-edit echoes uncover a language-like seed skeleton? (pre-registration)

Written before any h49 computing. No translation claims. Do not edit this file after it is committed.

## Question
`h47` and `h48` showed that MS 408's folio clustering and within-folio structure are well matched by a page-and-line-bounded copy-drift process where new stems are mutated down the page by one edit. If a real text, keyword list, or cipher stream seeded each folio and was then padded by local one-edit mutations, the mutated echoes would break word-to-word syntax (making $D_1 = \text{KN2/3} - \text{KN1}$ negative in raw MS 408). Does stripping same-folio exact repeats and one-edit echoes uncover a "seed skeleton" with positive word-order gain ($D_1 > 0$) or language-sized morpheme inventory (`h31`), or does the stripped MS skeleton match the stripped skeleton of the no-plaintext `h47` generator?

## Streams
Hands `1A`, `2B`, `3B` from `h30_lib.load_ms(hand)` (`ZL3b-n.txt`, comma as a word space, up to 10,500 tokens per hand, matching `h47`), compared against:
- **Synthetic no-plaintext generators (`h47`):** `page_line_copier_1A`, `page_line_copier_2B`, `page_line_copier_3B` (from `hypotheses/h47_page_line_generator/results/streams.json` or `h47_lib.generate_page_line_copier`).
- **Cipher + copy control:** `naibbe_boccaccio_swap` (Naibbe-Italian on Boccaccio + 5% suffix-swap copy, laid onto the 2B folio/line template).
- **Plain prose control:** `it_boccaccio` (first 10,500 tokens laid onto the 2B folio/line template).

## De-padding / seed-extraction filter (`extract_seeds`)
Applied identically inside each folio of a stream (using collapsed EVA units `h13_lib.SEG(w)` for EVA streams and characters for `it_boccaccio`):
1. **`local_W25` (windowed de-padding, $W = 25$):** Scan tokens $t = 0, 1, \dots, n_f - 1$ on the folio in reading order. Keep token $w_t$ as a **seed** iff no earlier token $w_s$ on the same folio within $1 \le t - s \le 25$ is identical ($u_t = u_s$) or at unit Levenshtein distance 1 ($\text{edit1}(u_t, u_s)$).
2. **`folio_comp` (whole-folio component seed):** Keep token $w_t$ as a **seed** iff no earlier token $w_s$ ($0 \le s < t$) anywhere on the same folio has $u_t = u_s$ or $\text{edit1}(u_t, u_s)$.

## Measures (computed on the raw stream and on both stripped seed streams)
1. **Retention rate (`keep_rate`):** Fraction of tokens retained after stripping.
2. **Held-out word-order gain (`D1_seed`):** 5-fold bifolium-grouped (or 5-block) Kneser–Ney bigram/trigram gain over unigrams on the surviving seed sequence, defined as in `h20_lib.dstats`: $D_1 = \text{bits}(\text{KN1}) - \min(\text{bits}(\text{KN2}), \text{bits}(\text{KN3}))$, so $D_1 > 0$ means word order helps prediction and $D_1 < 0$ means word order hurts. Also compute adjacent seed-word mutual information excess over 100 within-folio seed-order permutations (`mi_adj_z`, seed 49).
3. **Piece inventory (`morphs_per_type`, `h31`):** Bayesian / affix stem+suffix inventory ratio (`morphs_per_type` on the unique seed types, using the `h31` affix/segmentation estimator) to test whether stripping one-edit variants collapses the vocabulary to a smaller stem set or reveals a larger natural-language-like inventory ($\ge 0.18$).
4. **Cross-folio seed continuity (`adj_folio_cos`):** Mean cosine similarity (and one-edit neighbourhood overlap) between the seed set of folio $f$ and folio $f+1$ inside the same quire, vs 200 within-quire folio-order permutations (seed 49).

## Predictions
- **S1 (de-padding uncovers positive word-order syntax):** In at least two of the three MS hands (`1A`, `2B`, `3B`), stripping one-edit echoes (`local_W25` or `folio_comp`) flips $D_1$ from negative to strictly positive ($D_1 \ge +0.05$ bits/word) **and** exceeds the corresponding `page_line_copier` seed stream's $D_1$ by at least $+0.10$ bits/word.
- **S2 (sequential order among seeds inside a folio):** In at least two MS hands, the `folio_comp` seed stream has adjacent word-class (top-50 types / stems) bigram MI above the 95th percentile of 100 within-folio seed-order permutations (`mi_adj_z > 1.96`).
- **S3 (MS seed skeleton vs `h47` no-plaintext skeleton):** If MS 408's folio tokens were produced by the `h47` no-plaintext mechanism without a hidden seed message, then across Hands `1A`, `2B`, `3B` the `local_W25` retention rate, `D1_seed`, and `morphs_per_type` of MS will all lie within $\pm 0.08$ of `page_line_copier` (meaning S1 fails and the seed skeleton is indistinguishable from a random-stem generator).
